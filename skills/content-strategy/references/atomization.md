# Atomization: one idea, native cuts

Creating fresh content for every channel is how solo creators burn out. Pick the strongest 1 or 2 ideas per week (the
flagships) and rewrite each into a native cut per channel. A cut keeps the idea and the facts and rebuilds the hook,
length and rhythm for the channel.

In Writ this is the **Repurpose** feature: Split (one piece to several), Adapt (one piece, new medium), Combine
(several pieces to one). Use it so carried research stays approved and each cut has its lineage recorded.

## The pyramid

- **Flagship** (home channel): the full idea, with the proof and the story.
- **Derivative**: a thread, a newsletter issue or a short explainer video.
- **Micro**: single short posts, a carousel, a short video script, quote or stat graphics.

Aim for 3 to 5 cuts per flagship, not 15 to 25. More than that is padding.

## Steps

1. **State the core idea in one sentence.** Every cut must point back to it.
2. **Extract the spine:** the claim, the proof (a real number, example or story from the story bank), the correction or
   twist, and what the reader should do differently. Leave a line empty and mark it rather than invent it.
3. **Map cuts to channels** in a table, then draft only what the user wants:

| Channel | Format | Angle | Hook (draft) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

4. **Rebuild each hook** for its channel. A hook that works on the home channel rarely survives a short-text post or a
   video's first seconds.
5. **Adapt, do not trim.**
   - Home channel: room for a short story, whitespace, one concrete detail per beat.
   - Short-text channel: compress to the sharpest claim. A thread unrolls the argument one beat per post, each able to
     stand alone.
   - Carousel: hook on slide one, one point per slide in very few words, last slide a clear next step.
   - Short video script: AI-assisted or screen-recorded unless the subject says otherwise (see `channels.md`). Scene by
     scene: narration line (spoken rhythm), visual, text overlay. Hook in the first scene, three beats at most, a close.
   - Explainer video: a promise in the title, the answer early, one worked example.
   - Newsletter: the idea at full depth with its sources.
6. **Stagger release** over a week or two (flagship first, cuts after) instead of posting everything at once.
7. **Draft in the subject's voice** through the normal Writ piece workflow: `{{voice}}`, the format profile for the
   channel, then the grammar and fact checks.

## Quality check

Review each set of cuts on four questions. Answer in words, with a quote or a pointer as evidence. Do not turn it into
a number.

| Question | Passes when |
|---|---|
| Is each cut native to its channel? | Written for how that channel is read, not the same text with different line breaks |
| Does each cut have its own hook? | A distinct, specific first line per channel |
| Is it coherent? | One core idea; any single cut gives the whole point |
| Does it fit the goal? | Each cut reinforces the positioning and does not overreach `{{off_limits}}` |

## Rules

- Keep facts and numbers identical across cuts. Repurposing changes delivery, not meaning.
- Strip artefacts from the source channel: hashtag walls, "link in bio", handles that do not exist on the target.
- Never present AI-generated media as filmed or produced by the subject. Label as the platform requires.
- Reshare evergreen winners later with a new hook instead of writing from scratch.
