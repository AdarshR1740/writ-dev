# Ideation: backlog, matrix, ranking

Goal: a ranked list of ideas the subject can actually write, each tied to a pillar and backed by real material.

## Idea sources (in priority order)

1. **Story bank.** Insight cards from the foundation stage. Highest value because no one else has them.
2. **Work in progress.** What the subject is building or learning this week.
3. **Reader questions.** Questions from `{{icp}}`, prospects or peers, and comments on past posts.
4. **Live trends.** Run through Writ's research step so every item is reviewed. Verify the publish date of every item,
   drop anything outside the window you set (default 7 days), and never invent links or dates.
5. **Other people's content.** Reactions to a post or article in the field, with a real point of view.

## Pillars by formats matrix

For each pillar, write one specific headline for each of eight formats. A cell is a headline, not a theme ("a 3-line
check that catches thin pages", not "SEO tips").

| Format | What it does |
|---|---|
| Actionable | Teaches one thing, step by step |
| Motivational | A story of someone doing something hard in the field |
| Analytical | Why something works the way it does |
| Contrarian | Goes against common advice and backs it up |
| Observation | A quiet trend the subject has noticed |
| X vs Y | Compares two tools, methods or approaches |
| Present vs Future | Where things are now and a reasoned prediction |
| Listicle | Resources, mistakes or lessons |

Rules: no cell may be reusable under another pillar. Cells that depend on a number or story the subject has not given
are marked *needs input*, never filled with a plausible one.

## Information-gain check

Before an idea enters the backlog, write one sentence: **what does this add that the top posts on the topic do not?**
Original data, a real example, a working template, a clearer answer, or a position the subject is willing to defend. If
the sentence cannot be written, drop or rework the idea.

## Ranking

Ranking decides the order of work. It is not a quality score. Use plain judgements, each with a one-line reason, on
four questions:

| Question | Weight (starting point, adjust per `{{goal}}`) |
|---|---|
| Does it show real work or judgement that `{{icp}}` would respect? | High |
| Would the target reader stop and read? Is the hook specific? | Medium to high |
| Would someone who lands on the subject's profile afterwards feel more confident? | Medium |
| Is the material already in hand? | Low |

Output a ranked table of the top 10: pillar, format, source (story bank, trend, and so on), one-line hook, the reason
for its rank. Flag the 1 or 2 flagship ideas worth turning into several channel cuts.

For each flagship, also draft the **reader contract** for the piece: the one question it answers and 2 to 4 expectations
specific enough to fail.

## Balance check

Across any four-week window: no pillar above about 60% of posts; at most one promotional post per week; at least one
post that shows a real result and one that shows a real mistake or correction. Skip a slot rather than post to fill it.
