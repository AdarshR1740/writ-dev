# Foundation: positioning, pillars, story bank

Do this stage once, then revisit quarterly or when the subject's situation changes (new role, launch, new service).

## 1. Positioning line

Formula: **[who it helps] + [outcome] + [how or angle]**, written so most competitors could not claim it.

Start from `{{positioning}}`. If it names several lanes, offer 2 to 3 candidate lines and a recommendation. Do not pick
for the user. A brand that shouts every lane equally is blurry, so ask which lane leads for the next six months.

Test each candidate: **could a competitor write this sentence?** If yes, sharpen it. This is the same insight test Writ
uses on cards.

## 2. Pillars (3 to 4)

Pillars are durable themes, not topics of the week. Each must (a) prove something `{{icp}}` cares about and (b) be
something the subject can produce from real experience.

Offer hypotheses to confirm, never facts:

| Hypothesis | What it proves | Source material |
|---|---|---|
| `{{pillar_1}}` | | |
| `{{pillar_2}}` | | |
| `{{pillar_3}}` | | |

Propose a split of content across pillars with reasoning, shaped by `{{goal}}`, and let the user adjust.

## 3. Proof inventory

List what the subject can actually point to. Only confirmed items go in.

| Asset | Link or location | Can be public? | Result that can be stated |
|---|---|---|---|

Ask once per asset about numbers: measured how, over what period. Leave blank when unknown. Data cards are the natural
home for confirmed figures (value, unit, period, sample size, source).

## 4. Off-limits list

Build it from `{{off_limits}}` and confirm with the user. Typical categories:

- **Claims the subject cannot back up** (skills, results, clients, tools they have not used).
- **Capabilities they do not have**, stated as a list of named gaps.
- **Disclosure rules** (ghostwritten work flagged as such, AI-generated media labelled).
- **Confidential material** (salaries, private views of past employers, names of clients or colleagues without
  permission, internal-only cards).
- **Topics the subject prefers not to write about.**

Save the confirmed list in the strategy notes document so every later stage reads the same list.

## 5. Story bank (short interview)

Posts need specifics. A plan made without them fills up with themes nobody can write. Interview the subject for raw
material before planning. Budget 15 to 30 minutes, resumable.

Rules:

- **One question at a time.** Stacked questions get one answer.
- **Press every soft answer once.** "We improved traffic" becomes "by how much, measured how, over what period?" Once,
  not twice.
- **Never invent or smooth over a gap.** Leave it empty and mark the section thin.
- **Keep the subject's wording** where it is vivid. Paraphrase loses what made it usable.
- **Stop when they flag a limit** and record it under off-limits.

Sections to fill:

1. Work they are proud of, and the number behind it
2. Turning points: a belief held a year ago and no longer held, and what changed it
3. Scars: what went wrong and what it taught
4. Positions: something they think is true that peers dispute, and what holding that view costs
5. Stories they already tell out loud (pre-tested)
6. Build-in-public material: decisions and lessons they are happy to share

Save each answer as an **insight card** (type story, opinion, lesson or data point; public or internal; source: who,
where, when). Cards are the story bank, so nothing has to be re-entered later.

## 6. Profile alignment check

If outreach or traffic sends people to a profile or page, check it against the positioning line: headline, About,
featured items, banner, pinned or top posts. They should say the same thing. List gaps as a prioritised change list
with copy-paste text, not an edited file.
